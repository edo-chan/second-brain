# Flask Guidance

Read `intake.md` first for personalized flask advice. Inspect the actual flask
items, enchantments, charges, duration, and configuration rather than treating
the slot as permanently active.

## Assign A Job To Every Slot

Possible jobs include:

- recovery or emergency recovery;
- armour, evasion, suppression, maximum resistance, or another defensive layer;
- movement speed and mobility;
- ailment, bleed, corrupted blood, curse, poison, or stun handling;
- offensive exposure, penetration, critical scaling, or damage;
- build-enabling utility unique to the flask.

Avoid two flasks whose main effects do not stack meaningfully unless their
suffixes, uptime, or alternating use justify the duplication.

## Evaluate Modifiers

Useful modifier families depend on the build and current patch, but commonly
include charge recovery, reduced charges used, increased duration, gain charges
on hit or crit where reliable, increased armour or evasion, movement speed,
cast or attack speed, and ailment or curse handling.

Verify whether identical suffix effects from multiple flasks stack. Distinguish
immunity, reduced effect, avoidance, and removal; they solve different states.
Do not rely on a conditional flask suffix as the build's only protection when
charges are unavailable during the target content.

## Check Uptime

- Compare charge generation, charges consumed, duration, and boss downtime.
- Treat mapping-on-kill uptime separately from isolated boss uptime.
- Verify automation enchantment triggers and whether they can overlap or waste
  charges.
- Disable the flask in PoB when testing the build's permanent defensive floor.

An empty flask slot is an opportunity only after confirming it is truly unused
in the active item set and not disabled by a build mechanic.
