# MTG Arena Drafting

Use this reference for Draft picks, Limited deck construction, live-game
screenshots, and postmortems. Refresh set-specific archetypes, card text, and
performance data online; do not carry a pick order from one set into another.

## Refresh The Format Model

Before advising on a new set or returning to a changed format:

- verify the format, queue, set mechanics, and legal card pool with current
  official sources;
- consult current Limited data for color-pair performance, individual cards,
  and sample size;
- use experienced Limited analysis to understand why the data looks that way;
- record the date and evidence class when a claim depends on the current metagame.

Do not sort picks mechanically by one win-rate column. Opening-hand win rate,
games-in-hand win rate, improvement-when-drawn, average taken-at, archetype fit,
and sample size answer different questions. A strong card in successful decks
may still be wrong for the current pool, curve, or mana.

## Maintain A Pool Ledger

For each draft screenshot or pick, maintain the current counts for:

- colors and fixing;
- creatures and reliable creature equivalents;
- two-mana plays and three-mana stabilization;
- hard, conditional, and tempo interaction;
- card advantage and filtering;
- synergy enablers and payoffs;
- five-plus-mana cards and finishers.

Use visible evidence only. If the screenshot omits part of the pool, label the
recommendation provisional rather than inventing missing cards.

## Make Each Pick Deliberately

Evaluate the serious candidates in this order:

1. **Floor and power:** Is the card effective without drawing a specific
   partner, and how much does it affect the battlefield?
2. **Deck need:** Does it repair the curve, body count, interaction, fixing, or
   win condition that is currently thin?
3. **Lane evidence:** Are repeated useful cards indicating that these colors
   are open, or is the signal only one late multicolor card?
4. **Mana cost:** Can the likely mana base cast it when it matters? Treat early
   double-pip cards and splashes as real costs.
5. **Synergy:** Count the enablers and payoffs already owned; do not award full
   synergy value to a hoped-for future pool.
6. **Replaceability:** Prefer a scarce effect such as efficient removal or a
   strong two-drop when the alternative role is easy to fill later.

Lead with the pick. Mention the runner-up only when the decision is close and
state what new information would reverse the choice.

Stay flexible during the opening picks, then commit as repeated signals and the
pool's actual strengths converge. In the final pack, fill structural holes
before taking speculative sideboard cards or another expensive payoff.

## Build The Best 40

Request or inspect the entire pool before claiming a final build. Sort by mana
value and tag every nonland by role. Start from these defaults, then deviate for
a concrete archetype reason:

- exactly 40 cards;
- 17 lands, adjusted for curve, fixing, and real mana acceleration;
- roughly 14-17 creatures or reliable creature equivalents;
- at least five cards that affect the battlefield by turn two, plus additional
  three-mana stabilization;
- several interaction spells, ideally including at least two answers that can
  handle a large creature or engine permanent when the pool supports them;
- a small, credible top end rather than a hand full of expensive value spells.

These are warning thresholds, not automatic cuts. A supported spells deck may
use fewer creatures; a low-curve deck may use fewer lands. State the mechanism
that makes the exception safe.

Count interaction by what it actually does:

- **hard answer:** destroys, exiles, or otherwise removes the threat for good;
- **conditional answer:** works only against a subset of threats or with setup;
- **tempo:** taps, stuns, or bounces but leaves the opponent's card available;
- **combat trick:** requires combat and a suitable creature.

Do not call tempo or combat tricks permanent removal. Tempo is valuable only
when the deck applies pressure or converts the delay into a decisive swing.
Card draw is not stabilization: first establish how the deck survives the early
battlefield, then count its card-flow advantages.

Write the finished deck's plan in one sentence. Every included synergy payoff
must have a realistic enabler count, and every narrow enabler must contribute
when the payoff is absent. Prefer at least two paths to winning instead of a
deck that collapses when its best rare is answered.

## Build The Mana From Requirements

Count colored sources, not just lands. Note the turn each colored pip is needed,
how many lands enter tapped, and whether fixing consumes mana or requires a
creature to survive. A splash needs both adequate fixing and a payoff worth the
consistency cost; basic-land totals alone do not justify a third color.

Prepared cards, split cards, alternative costs, treasure, and cost reduction
can change the functional curve. Count what the deck can actually cast on each
turn rather than only the printed mana value.

## Apply The Prismari Lesson

Secrets of Strixhaven Prismari wants Opus creatures on the battlefield before
the instant or sorcery that rewards them. Five-plus-mana spells can produce the
larger Opus trigger, but too many of them leave the deck unable to contest early
creatures. Balance the package in this order:

1. early bodies and stabilization;
2. efficient or scalable interaction;
3. Opus payoffs that are acceptable creatures on their own;
4. card draw and a limited number of expensive spells or finishers.

Tap, stun, and bounce effects do not solve a lifegain engine or a large trampler
permanently. A Prismari pool without enough hard answers must either add a real
clock or change colors; adding more draw spells does not repair that weakness.

## Learn Without Keeping A Battle Record

When reviewing a game, distinguish drafting, building, play, and variance. A
final-board screenshot establishes the loss mechanism, not necessarily the
first mistake. Identify the earliest useful intervention and distill only the
reusable drafting or construction rule. Do not maintain a match-by-match win or
loss log unless Ed explicitly asks for one.

For an opposing synergy board, identify the repeatable enabler, the payoff, and
the threshold after which ordinary one-for-one or tempo play no longer works.
Stop the payoff before its threshold when possible; otherwise remove the
enabler early. Avoid spending premium interaction on interchangeable bodies
while the engine remains.

## Coach Live Screenshots

Lead with the exact action while the decision is live: spell, target, attackers
or blockers, and mana to preserve. Then give the shortest reason needed. If no
action is currently available, say what to hold up and the trigger or spell
that should cause Ed to respond.
