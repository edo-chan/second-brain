# Abstract Algebra Diagnostic

Use the diagnostic to choose a starting point, not to shorten the traditional
course. Prior exposure changes pacing and problem difficulty; it does not
remove a topic whose proofs or examples are no longer operational.

## Confidence map

Rate each item from 0 to 4 using the scale in the
[project diagnostic](../diagnostic.md).

- **A1.** Functions, equivalence relations, and proof techniques
- **A2.** Group axioms and standard examples
- **A3.** Subgroups, cyclic groups, cosets, and Lagrange's theorem
- **A4.** Homomorphisms, normal subgroups, quotients, and isomorphism theorems
- **A5.** Group actions and finite-group structure
- **A6.** Rings, ideals, quotient rings, and the Chinese remainder theorem
- **A7.** Polynomial rings, divisibility, and irreducibility
- **A8.** Field extensions and finite fields
- **A9.** Galois theory
- **A10.** Linear maps, duality, bilinear forms, modules, and tensors
- **A11.** Multivariate polynomials and computational algebra
- **A12.** Elliptic curves and pairings

## Adaptive probe bank

Answer only the probes selected after the confidence map is reviewed.

### AP1: Structure

Define a group and give one example and one non-example. For the non-example,
identify the first axiom that fails.

### AP2: Subgroups and cosets

List the subgroups of \(\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z\) under addition and explain why
their sizes divide 12.

### AP3: Homomorphisms and quotients

For a homomorphism \(f:G\to H\), explain why \(\ker f\) is normal and what the
first isomorphism theorem says.

### AP4: Rings and ideals

Distinguish a subring from an ideal. Explain why ideals, rather than arbitrary
subrings, are the objects used to form quotient rings.

### AP5: Polynomial quotients

Determine when \(\mathbb F_p[x]/(f(x))\) is a field and construct one small
example.

### AP6: Finite fields

Explain why every nonzero element of a finite field lies in a finite
multiplicative group and what this implies about exponentiation.

### AP7: Linear structure

Given a linear map, distinguish its matrix, kernel, image, eigenvectors, and
minimal polynomial without treating them as the same object.

### AP8: Polynomial reasoning

Prove that a nonzero polynomial of degree \(d\) over a field has at most \(d\)
roots, and identify the step that fails over a general ring with zero divisors.

### AP9: Elliptic curves

Explain why the points of an elliptic curve can form an abelian group and what
additional work is needed beyond writing down the curve equation.
