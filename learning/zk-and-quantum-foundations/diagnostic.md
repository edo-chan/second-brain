# Two-Track Diagnostic

The diagnostics select starting points for the abstract-algebra spine,
zero-knowledge proof systems, and quantum cryptography. They do not assume the
tracks are equally advanced.

## Step 1: Confidence maps

Complete the active confidence map without references. Begin with algebra:

1. [Algebra confidence map](algebra/diagnostic.md#confidence-map)
2. [ZK confidence map](zk/diagnostic.md#confidence-map)
3. [Quantum confidence map](quantum-cryptography/diagnostic.md#confidence-map)

Use the same scale in all three:

- **0 - New:** I have not studied it.
- **1 - Familiar:** I recognize the terms.
- **2 - Usable:** I can solve standard exercises with references.
- **3 - Operational:** I can derive and explain it without references.
- **4 - Fluent:** I can implement it, prove the relevant claims, or teach it.

## Step 2: Adaptive probes

After reviewing a confidence map, answer only selected probes near the claimed
boundary. Ask one rating or probe at a time. Stop once the earliest missing
dependency is located instead of administering the entire bank.

## Step 3: Starting lessons

Record these results in [progress.md](progress.md):

- strongest existing foundation in the active track;
- earliest missing dependency in the active track;
- first algebra lesson;
- first ZK lesson;
- first quantum-cryptography lesson;
- whether the tracks should alternate evenly or one should temporarily lead.

## Initial response format

Start conversationally with A1 and continue one item at a time. Record
preferred session size and experiment tools when they become clear rather
than presenting a large intake form.
