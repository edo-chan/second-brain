# Learning Session Continuity

Use this guide with `ed-teacher` when resuming an interrupted lesson,
reconciling session evidence, or maintaining learning records. The teacher's
session method owns pacing and assessment; its evolution method owns changes
to teaching behavior and curricula.

## Recover The Actual Stopping Point

Read the active project's records and the relevant conversation evidence.
Locate the last concept attempted, the exact unanswered question or unfinished
derivation, the help already supplied, and the next step recorded at the time.
If the conversation supplies newer evidence, reconcile it with the record.

Resume with one brief orientation and the pending learner step. Do not restart
the whole diagnostic merely because a task or context changed. If the precise
stopping point is unavailable, say what the records establish and use one
focused retrieval probe to recover the boundary.

Distinguish an interruption from a demonstrated misunderstanding. A pause or an
unanswered prompt does not establish failure, completion, or lost mastery.

## Preserve Evidence When Recording Progress

Use the project's existing progress structure. For a meaningful update, retain:

- the concept or checkpoint and date;
- the learner's actual attempt or a precise account of the decisive step;
- whether hints, notes, worked solutions, or other assistance were available;
- the evidence level supported under the teacher's session method;
- the unresolved question and one next step.

An assistant's worked solution is instructional material, not the learner's
attempt. A learner repeating a displayed answer does not by itself establish
independent reconstruction. Preserve the assistance context when classifying
the result; a later retrieval or transfer check can supply stronger evidence.

Record the same interaction once. When revising an assessment, preserve the
earlier observation and explain the correction rather than fabricating another
session or silently replacing its history. Keep notes, questions, and progress
in their existing owning files; do not create a parallel tracking system for
every lesson.

## Maintain The System Without Advancing The Learner

For a request to reorganize skills, improve the teacher, or edit a curriculum,
apply the evolution method to the requested change. Update reusable guidance
or sequence at its owner. Do not mark exercises attempted, checkpoints passed,
or topics mastered unless the interaction contains the corresponding learner
evidence.

Preserve unrelated local learner edits. If progress records disagree or the
active project is unclear, resolve only what current evidence supports before
writing progress; continue independent guidance maintenance in the meantime.
